## Building the foundation for an ecosystem

Mingo Platform is the foundation of Mingo, an independent software ecosystem I'm building to create, manage, and deliver digital products for businesses and individuals.

Rather than developing isolated applications, the vision behind Mingo is to establish a shared platform where products can coexist while maintaining their own business logic, capabilities, and user experiences.

The platform provides the foundation for identity management, organizations, product discovery, subscriptions, access control, and service provisioning.

## The challenge

Building multiple software products introduces challenges that extend beyond individual applications.

Each product needs its own domain and functionality, but several concerns are shared across the ecosystem:

- Identity and authentication.
- Organization and personal account management.
- Roles, permissions, and authorization.
- Product catalogs and subscription plans.
- Feature availability and usage limits.
- Application provisioning and lifecycle management.
- Consistent experiences across independent products.

The challenge is to centralize these shared capabilities without creating unnecessary dependencies between products.

## Architecture and engineering

Mingo Platform follows a modular architecture designed around clear domain boundaries and maintainability.

The backend is built with NestJS, TypeScript, TypeORM, and PostgreSQL, applying Clean Architecture principles to separate business rules, application use cases, infrastructure, and HTTP interfaces.

The frontend uses Next.js with the App Router, providing public product experiences, organizational management, and administrative interfaces.

The architecture emphasizes:

- Clear separation of domain, application, infrastructure, and presentation layers.
- Explicit use cases and business rules.
- Transactional consistency for critical operations.
- Database migrations and controlled schema evolution.
- Automated testing and continuous integration.
- Independent evolution of platform capabilities.

## Identity and access management

One of the central engineering challenges has been designing identity management for different types of users.

Mingo distinguishes between three identity contexts:

- **Business accounts:** Users belonging to an organization.
- **Personal accounts:** Individuals using products independently.
- **Platform staff:** Internal administrators responsible for managing the ecosystem.

These contexts are designed to remain independent, with separate authentication boundaries, permissions, and account lifecycles.

Authentication is built around Keycloak and OpenID Connect, while application-level authorization remains controlled by the platform's business rules.

The system uses role-based access control, permission evaluation, and explicit authorization decisions to protect organizational resources.

## Products, plans, and subscriptions

Mingo includes a product catalog that allows different applications to be registered and managed within the ecosystem.

Each product can define its own subscription plans, capabilities, and usage limits.

The subscription architecture incorporates:

- Product and plan management.
- Versioned plan configurations.
- Feature entitlements.
- Usage limits and enforcement.
- Subscription lifecycle management.
- Subscription events and overrides.

A central entitlement engine evaluates which capabilities are available to an account based on its active subscriptions and applicable business rules.

This allows products to share commercial infrastructure without duplicating subscription logic.

## Building independent products

Mingo Platform is designed to support multiple applications while preserving their individual domains.

Two products currently shaping the ecosystem are:

### Mingo Billing

A web-first billing and commercial management product for businesses.

It is being developed to integrate with Mingo's organizational identity and subscription infrastructure while maintaining its own billing domain.

### Mingo Tribia

A personal financial and tax organization product concept for independent professionals in the Dominican Republic.

Its planned architecture introduces personal accounts as a distinct identity context, independent from organizational accounts.

Together, these products influence how the platform handles identity, product access, and different types of customers.

## Infrastructure and delivery

Beyond application development, Mingo also involves designing the infrastructure required to operate and deploy its services.

The development and deployment environment incorporates:

- Docker and containerized applications.
- PostgreSQL for relational persistence.
- Keycloak for identity and authentication.
- GitHub Actions for continuous integration.
- Automated linting, type checking, testing, and builds.
- VPS-based infrastructure and application deployment through Dokploy.

The objective is to establish a reproducible development and deployment workflow that can evolve alongside the ecosystem.
